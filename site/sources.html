Sources & method

This is a map of beginnings, not of today's believers. Dates of origin are often disputed; the map says so rather than hiding it.

Dates

60 of 91 start dates are approximate and are written with "c." On the timeline their start dot is hollow. Each tradition's page explains its date in words, including where tradition and historians disagree (for example Zarathustra, the Buddha, or Shinto). Years are BCE/CE; there is no year 0. Living traditions run to 2026, the year this site was built, and stay open-ended.

Places

Each origin is a single latitude and longitude. 57 are a specific site (a city, a mountain, a temple); 34 are a region, drawn with a soft halo, because the tradition has no single birthplace. Spread routes mark dated places a tradition reached, each drawn as a great-circle line from the place it came from. They are illustrative milestones, not complete histories.

The folded time axis

Every chart uses the same three-part axis: 3500 BCE–500 BCE takes 24% of the width, 500 BCE–1500 takes 38%, and 1500–2026 takes 38%. So a year after 1500 is drawn 9.0× wider than a year before 500 BCE, and 3.8× wider than a year between. Lengths compare honestly only inside one stretch. The folds are drawn as zigzag seams.

Corrections to the earlier version

  • Sikhism began c. 1499 with Guru Nanak's revelation, not 1469 (his birth year).
  • "Orthodox Judaism" as a named movement dates from the early 19th century, not 2000 BCE.
  • Roman religion ended with the bans of 391–392; the Edict of Thessalonica (380) made Christianity official but did not ban other cults.
  • Shinto's 500 BCE start had no source; codified Shinto is dated from the 8th century.
  • Christianity's start is c. 30 CE; there is no year 0.
